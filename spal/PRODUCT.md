# Spal: Product Definition

Nov 11, 2026 · Joshua Jumbo

This is the source of truth for what Spal is. Read it before planning or building anything.

## What Spal is

**Spal is the assistant you can't afford to hire: a buddy that knows your business, helps you sell more, and handles the work you don't have hands for.**

The buddy is who Spal is. More sales is what the buddy is working towards. Records, debts and insights all serve that goal, and none of them is the product on its own.

The model is a good shop assistant. She is valued because she knows the customers by name, remembers who owes, notices what is running low, and speaks up when something is off. Spal should do the same.

## Who it's for

The first user is someone already running a shop in the informal sector: street food, retail, a boutique, a small restaurant, a mall shop. People who have not started yet come later.

What she wants, in her order of importance:

1. More sales, above everything else
2. Existing customers who come back often
3. Little or no debt owed to her
4. New customers
5. A wider network to advertise to
6. Access to financing

Her main constraint is that she is short-staffed and busy serving customers. She will not do data entry, and she may not be comfortable with technology. Anything Spal asks of her has to cost less effort than it returns.

## How Spal behaves

These rules are what make Spal feel like a buddy instead of a tool she has to operate.

- **It speaks first.** It greets her with what matters today, the way an assistant would when she walks in.
- **It talks like a person.** "Mama Chidi hasn't come in two weeks, should I check on her?" is right. "Customer retention down 12%" is wrong.
- **It speaks her language.** She can talk or type the way she normally does, and Spal answers the same way.
- **It remembers.** She never has to tell it the same thing twice.
- **It asks before it acts.** It suggests and she approves. As trust builds, she can let it handle more alone.
- **It's on her side.** It celebrates a good day and is honest about a bad one, without lecturing.
- **It stays out of the way.** If there is nothing useful to say, it says nothing.

The test for any feature: would a smart, trusted shop assistant do this? If so, it belongs. If it feels like software, it doesn't.

## The core loop

Everything in Spal comes down to one loop that repeats every day.

1. **She tells it.** By voice, text, or a photo of a receipt or her notebook, she says what happened: sales, spending, who owes.
2. **Spal keeps it.** It does the books, debts, stock and customer records in the background. She never sees a form.
3. **Spal tells her.** It gives her something she didn't know or would have forgotten: "You made ₦42k today, ₦8k more than last Tuesday."
4. **Spal does it.** She says yes and it sends the reminder, the restock list, or the message to her regulars.

Each time round, Spal knows her business better and its advice gets sharper. That is how it grows with her, and she does nothing extra to make it happen.

## How recording works

Spal supports recording as it happens and catching up at closing, with no setting or mode to choose. Catch-up is what most owners will realistically do, so it is the safety net. Recording as it happens stays available because habits change.

The big button is always there, and the closing conversation always happens. Spal fits the closing conversation to the day she had:

| Her day | What Spal says at closing |
| --- | --- |
| She recorded nothing | "How was today?" She talks through it or snaps her notebook page. |
| She recorded some | "I have 6 sales, ₦21,000 so far. Anything I missed?" |
| She recorded everything | "11 sales, ₦38,500. Does that sound right?" |

Spal is useful at every level of detail and never demands more:

| What she tells it | What Spal can do |
| --- | --- |
| "I made about ₦40k today" | Daily and weekly totals, good days against slow days |
| What sold and who took credit | Best sellers, restock hints, debt reminders |
| Who bought what | Bringing customers back, which is where the extra sales come from |

Three rules sit on top of this:

- **Spal earns more detail by showing what it's worth.** "If you tell me who buys, I can remind them when their usual is back in stock."
- **Spal follows her habits.** If she always catches up at 8pm, it checks in at 8pm. After three quiet days it says "Welcome back, want to catch me up?" with no guilt and no broken streak.
- **Credit sales are captured as they happen wherever possible.** A debt forgotten by evening is money lost, so this is the first habit Spal encourages.

## The first day and first week

Spal has to give something back on day one, because record apps lose her by asking for work before they return anything. The example below follows Amaka, who sells provisions and has a helper some days.

1. **She opens Spal (under a minute).** No sign-up wall and no tour. Spal asks her name, what she sells, and what to call her shop. Then: "I'm here to help Amaka Stores sell more. When you make a sale today, just tell me."
2. **Her first sale.** She taps the big button and says "Sold two tins of milk and one sugar, ₦4,500." Spal confirms in a line and asks who bought it. She can answer or skip.
3. **During the day.** Spal stays quiet. She records a credit sale ("Blessing took rice, she'll pay Friday"), an expense ("Bought fuel, ₦3,000"), or asks a question ("How much have I made today?").
4. **Closing time.** Spal speaks first: "Ready to close? Did I miss anything?" Then her summary: "Today you made ₦38,500 from 11 sales. You spent ₦3,000. Milk sold the most. Blessing owes ₦12,000."
5. **Saving.** "Should I save this so we continue tomorrow?" She creates her account here, once she has something worth keeping.
6. **Next morning.** "Good morning, Amaka. Yesterday was ₦38,500. Let's beat it today."

The first week has three payoffs:

| Day | What she gets |
| --- | --- |
| Day 1 | Her first closing summary |
| Day 3 | Her first small insight, such as her best seller or busiest hour |
| Day 7 | "This week you made X, spent Y, and kept Z. Two customers owe you." |

On the day a debt is due, Spal makes its first offer to act: "Blessing is due to pay ₦12,000 today. Should I send her a reminder?"

If she feels by day 7 that the app knows her shop, she stays.

## The app

The app has three places, so someone who doesn't understand tech can use it in a few taps.

| Place | What it's for |
| --- | --- |
| Today | The one to three things Spal wants her to know or approve |
| Talk | One big button to tell Spal something or ask it anything |
| My business | Her history, customers and money, for when she wants to look |

She does not pick a stage or level in onboarding. Spal works out where her business is from her activity and adjusts what it says.

## What Spal does for her, in order

Spal starts with what it can deliver alone from her own data, and adds the rest as users and history build up.

| Her need | What Spal does | When it's possible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customers coming back | Remembers each customer and what they buy, then nudges them: "Your usual is back in stock" | Day one, needs only her own customers |
| Little or no debt | Tracks who owes and sends polite reminders in her name | Day one |
| Attracting new customers | Makes her posts, flyers and WhatsApp status from what she sells | Early, with no network needed |
| Advertising to a strong network | Shows her shop to other Spal users and their customers | Later, needs many users |
| Financing | Her Spal history becomes proof that her business is creditworthy | Later, needs months of records and a lending partner |

Repeat customers are the starting point. It was a strong theme in the 15 shop interviews, and it is the quickest way to show her more money. It also shapes what she records: "sold rice to Mama Chidi" instead of "sold rice".

Financing is the long game. It gives her a reason to keep recording for months, and it fits charging through the wallet or transfer fees instead of an upfront subscription.

Community and mentorship with other entrepreneurs also come later. The buddy has to work alone first.

Messages to her customers need care. She approves each one at first, and it must sound like her, or it will feel like spam and hurt the relationships she depends on.

## What Spal is not

- **Not a dashboard.** Opening it shows what matters today. Charts are there only if she asks.
- **Not a chatbot.** A chatbot waits for a question. Spal speaks first.
- **Not a course.** Its advice is about her shop and her numbers.
- **Not a records app.** Kippa, Bumpa and Odoo are tools she has to operate. In Spal the records happen as a byproduct of talking to it.

## Open questions

- [ ] Which action does Spal take on her behalf first: chasing debts, bringing back regulars, or restocking?
- [ ] How do messages reach her customers (WhatsApp, SMS), and how do customers agree to receive them?
- [ ] Which languages does Spal speak at launch (English, Pidgin, others)?
- [ ] How reliable is voice and notebook-photo capture for these users, and what is the fallback when it gets something wrong?
- [ ] Does Spal need to work with poor or no network connection?
- [ ] Pricing was not discussed in the interviews. Does the wallet and transfer-fee model hold up when tested with shop owners?
- [ ] When do people who haven't started a business yet come in, and what does Spal do for them?
